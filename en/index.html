Ethics

A subject that doesn't hand out ready-made answers, but teaches how to ask. Why do we consider something good? Who decided what is right? And what happens when two right things collide?

The first question: what are we actually studying?

Every day you make dozens of decisions that are neither mathematical nor technical: will you copy on a test, will you tell a friend a truth that hurts, will you stay silent when someone in class is left out? Nobody gives you a formula for that. But that doesn't mean we can't think about it seriously.

Definition

Ethics

Ethics is a philosophical discipline (a field of study) that investigates human moral action: it asks what is good, what is right, how we judge, and how we can justify our judgements. The word comes from the Greek ethos: custom, character, disposition.

Ethics does not prescribe. It examines what has been prescribed.

Definition

Morality

Morality is the set of values, norms and rules that actually hold in a community and that people live by: what a given society considers good, acceptable and shameful. From the Latin mos, moris: custom, habit.

Morality tells us what to do. Ethics asks why exactly that.

Morality is the language a community uses to speak about the good. Ethics is the grammar that examines that language.

Ethics or morality? A difference worth knowing

Students (and adults) mix these two words up all the time. In short: morality is what we have, ethics is what we think about it.

 MORALITYETHICS
What is it? A system of values and norms a community actually lives by A field of study (a philosophical discipline) that examines those values
Key question “What is allowed and what isn't?” “Why would that be good? Can we justify it?”
Relationship The object of study The one doing the studying
Where it comes from Spontaneously, through upbringing, religion, custom, community Deliberately, through reflection, arguments and debate
How it changes Differs from era to era and from culture to culture Looks for reasons that hold beyond a single culture
Example “Where I'm from, you give up your seat on the tram to older people.” “Do we have a duty to help a stranger, and where does it come from?”
Comparison The language we speak The grammar that describes and examines that language
Did you know?

Both words originally mean the same thing: “custom”. Ethos is Greek, mos is its Latin translation. The difference in meaning isn't linguistic but philosophical: it appeared only when people began to distinguish what is done from what can be justified. Someone can be very moral without ever having heard of ethics, and someone can be a brilliant ethicist and a bad person.

🔄

A third concept: custom

Don't confuse morality with custom (taking off your hat in church) or with law (traffic regulations). Break a custom and you're impolite; break the law and the state punishes you; break a moral rule and your conscience bothers you.

⚖️

Moral ≠ legal

Slavery was legal for centuries, but it was never moral. Hiding persecuted people was illegal, but moral. That very gap is where ethics does its work.

🧠

Why do we learn this?

So that on life's most important questions we have not only feelings and habits, but also an argument: something we can state, defend and, if necessary, change.

Quick exercise: is this ethics or morality?

For each sentence, decide whether it describes morality (a value that is lived) or ethics (reflection on values).

Mini task

Write down one rule that everyone in your family or class follows, even though nobody knows where it came from. Then try to write two reasons for and two reasons against that rule. You've just moved from morality to ethics.
