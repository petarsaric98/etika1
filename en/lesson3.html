Lesson 3

The Individual and the Community

What happens when what an individual believes is right clashes with the will of those in power or of the community? Antigone and Prometheus: two who said “no” and paid the price.

Learning outcomes

After this lesson you should be able to: retell the myths of Antigone and Prometheus; explain the conflict between conscience and law; argue for both Antigone's and Creon's positions; explain Hegel's idea of a conflict between two justified rights; and recognise why a right action is not always legal or permitted.

1. Me or us?

Everyone lives in a community: a family, a class, a town, a country. A community has rules and an authority that enforces them, and without that we couldn't live together. But everyone also has a conscience, an inner sense of what is right.

Most of the time the two agree. But sometimes they collide. Then the individual has to choose: obey the rules of the community, or their own conscience? In this lesson we look at two myths that ask exactly that question.

👤

The individual

Has a conscience, their own values and responsibility for what they do. They can't hide behind “I was just following orders”.

🏛️

The community

Has laws and an authority that protect order, safety and equality. Without them everyone would do as they pleased, and the strongest would always win.

2. Antigone: conscience against the law

Antigone is Oedipus's daughter, so this story continues the one from the last lesson.

⚖️
Conscience vs. law

Background

After Oedipus went into exile, his sons Eteocles and Polynices agreed to rule Thebes in turns, one year each. When Polynices's turn came, Eteocles refused to hand over power. Polynices raised an army in another city and attacked his own Thebes. In the battle the brothers killed each other.

Creon's decree

The new king was Creon, Jocasta's brother. He ordered that Eteocles be buried with honours because he had defended the city. He declared Polynices a traitor for attacking his homeland. His body must remain unburied, to be eaten by dogs and birds, and whoever buries him will be punished with death.

Why was this so terrible?

For the ancient Greeks, an unburied soul could not enter the underworld and would wander forever. Burying the dead was a sacred duty, a law of the gods. Creon's decree wasn't just harsh; it touched what was most sacred.

Antigone's choice

Antigone asks her sister Ismene to help her bury their brother. Ismene refuses because she is afraid and believes women cannot go against the king. Antigone does it alone: she sprinkles earth over her brother's body and performs the rite. She is caught and brought before Creon.

Their conversation is the heart of the whole story:

👑

Creon

The law is the law and it applies to everyone. If the king gives in once, chaos will rule the city. A traitor doesn't deserve the same honours as a defender.

🌿

Antigone

Above the king's laws there are unwritten, eternal laws of the gods. No mortal has the right to forbid the burial of a brother. She would rather die than betray that duty.

🕯️
Tragedy

How it ends

Creon sentences Antigone to be walled up alive in a cave. His son Haemon, Antigone's fiancé, begs his father to relent. He tells him the whole city secretly supports Antigone. Creon won't listen.

Only when the blind prophet Tiresias warns him that the gods are angry does Creon change his mind, but too late. Antigone has hanged herself in the cave, Haemon kills himself beside her, and then Creon's wife Eurydice kills herself too. Creon is left alone and broken. The best-known version is Sophocles' tragedy Antigone.

Creon isn't simply evil either

The most interesting thing is that Creon defends order, stability and equality before the law, and these are values that matter to a community. That's why the philosopher Hegel said this is a conflict between two justified rights, that of the family and that of the state. The tragedy arises because neither side will give way.

Who are you in this story?

Each character in Antigone resolves the conflict between the individual and authority differently. Which one is closest to you?

3. Prometheus: the individual who sacrifices himself for everyone

🔥
Rebellion for the good of others

Who is Prometheus?

Prometheus was a titan, one of the old gods who came before the Olympians. His name means “the one who thinks ahead”. In some versions he made humans out of clay, and he was always on their side.

The first trick

When gods and humans were agreeing how to share a sacrifice, Prometheus divided an ox into two portions. In one he hid the meat under the ugly hide and stomach. In the other he put the bones under a fine layer of glistening fat. Zeus chose the nicer-looking portion: the bones. Ever since, humans eat the meat and burn the bones for the gods.

The theft of fire

Furious, Zeus withheld fire from humans. Without it they would be left in darkness and cold, with no cooked food, no crafts and no progress. Prometheus secretly stole fire from Olympus, hid it in a plant stalk and brought it to humans. Along with fire, he taught them skills: counting, writing, metalworking. Fire thus became the symbol of knowledge and civilisation.

The punishment

Zeus chained Prometheus to a rock in the Caucasus. Every day an eagle came and pecked at his liver, and overnight his liver grew back, so his suffering was endless. Despite this, Prometheus neither repented nor submitted to Zeus. After a long time he was freed by Heracles. The best-known version is Aeschylus's tragedy Prometheus Bound.

The prototype of the rebel and benefactor

Prometheus breaks the rules of those in power not for himself, but to help everyone. He is the prototype of the rebel and benefactor who pays the price for the progress of others.

A modern Prometheus

You work for a company and discover it is secretly poisoning the river the town drinks from. Your contract forbids you from revealing anything to the public. What do you do?

And what about humans? (Preview of the next topic)

Zeus punished humans separately: he sent them Pandora, the first woman, with a jar full of evils. That's already the next topic: good and evil.

4. Comparison: two who said “no”

Both myths ask the same question: what happens when what an individual believes is right clashes with the will of those in power or of the community?

 ANTIGONEPROMETHEUS
Who they defy Creon, the city's ruler Zeus, the supreme authority
In the name of Conscience and a family, “divine” duty The good of all humanity
What they risk Their own life Eternal suffering
Do they repent? No No
⚖️

Antigone

Shows the conflict between conscience and law. Neither side is simply evil: both defend something valuable.

🔥

Prometheus

Shows an individual who sacrifices himself for the community. He breaks the rules to help everyone, not himself.

A right action is not always legal or permitted. And an individual who stands up to those in power almost always pays a high price.

Final tasks

Choose one

1. Debate. Team Antigone vs. team Creon. Each team presents three arguments and anticipates one counter-argument from the other side. At the end the class votes: who was more convincing, and who was right? (They don't have to be the same!)

2. A letter (10 sentences). Write the letter Haemon sends his father Creon the day before Antigone is walled up. Try to persuade him with arguments, not just feelings.

3. Research. Find one real person, past or present, who, like Antigone or Prometheus, broke the rules of those in power out of conscience or for the good of others. What did they risk, and what did they achieve?
